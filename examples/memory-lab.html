Read. Reduce. Recall.

This page tests the first local-first memory cycle: read a passage, reduce it to up to three anchors, add one optional “In my words” thought, then recall before revealing the original.

ReadOpen a paragraph with VL and use Focus/Deep to isolate the thought.
ReduceSelect up to three short anchors. Then add one optional sentence in “In my words”.
RecallPress Recall. Use only the anchors first; reveal your thought and the original afterwards.
Charles Darwin · On the Origin of Species · 1859

Causal argument

As many more individuals of each species are born than can possibly survive, and as, consequently, there is a frequently recurring struggle for existence, it follows that any being, if it vary however slightly in any manner profitable to itself, under the complex and sometimes varying conditions of life, will have a better chance of surviving, and thus be naturally selected.

Try anchors such as “more individuals are born”, “struggle for existence”, and “better chance of surviving”. Then write the causal idea in one sentence of your own.

Jane Austen · Pride and Prejudice · 1813

Subtext

It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.

Reduce the sentence to cues that help you recover the social assumption and its exaggerated certainty, not merely the literal wording.

Лев Толстой · Анна Каренина · 1878

Thesis

Все счастливые семьи похожи друг на друга, каждая несчастливая семья несчастлива по-своему.

Сделайте anchors, затем сформулируйте тезис одной собственной фразой. После сохранения обновите страницу и снова откройте тот же абзац.
